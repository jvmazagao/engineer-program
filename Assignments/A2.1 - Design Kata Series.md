# A2.1 — Design Kata Series

← [[02 - Phase 2 - System Design]] · Catalog: [[Assignments.base]]

| 2 — System Design | end of week 26 | ✍️ written | ~12 h | peer+mentor |

## Why

Reading DDIA gives you the bricks; this gives you the building. Six designs is enough repetitions that requirements → estimates → diagram → failure modes becomes reflex instead of effort.

## Brief

One design per week, matching the phase schedule. Each design is a note under `Cohort/<you>/Designs/` with these sections, in this order:

1. **Requirements** — functional list + non-functional targets (latency, availability, consistency), and ≥3 assumptions stated explicitly
2. **Estimates** — back-of-the-envelope: QPS read/write, storage per year, bandwidth; show the arithmetic
3. **Diagram** — boxes and arrows (mermaid or Excalidraw), every box justified in a caption
4. **Data model** — tables/keys/indexes, partitioning or sharding choice if relevant
5. **Failure modes** — what breaks, what the user sees, how the design survives it (≥2 modes)
6. **Options considered** — 2–3 alternatives and why each lost

The six systems:

1. **URL shortener** (week 21) — decide on custom aliases, expiry, analytics
2. **News feed / timeline** (week 22) — fan-out on write vs. read; handle the celebrity user
3. **Order processing with retries + idempotency** (week 23) — at-least-once queue, outbox, idempotency keys, saga for the failure path
4. **Distributed rate limiter** (week 24) — pick an algorithm (token bucket, sliding window), handle multi-instance state
5. **Notification system** (week 25) — multi-channel (email/push/SMS), dedup, user preferences, retry without spamming
6. **One from your own domain** (week 26) — a system your company actually has; use real numbers where you can get them

Then swap: review two of your peer's designs, they review two of yours. Comments should change the design — "have you considered X" is fine; "+1 nice" is not.

## Tasks

- [ ] Create `Cohort/<you>/Designs/` with one note per system
- [ ] Designs 1–5 from the list above, each with all six sections
- [ ] Design 6 from your own domain, with real QPS/data numbers where obtainable
- [ ] Do estimates on paper before drawing the diagram; check them against anything real you know
- [ ] Swap with a peer: review 2 of their designs, receive 2 reviews of yours
- [ ] For each review comment: fix the design or write down why not
- [ ] Bring design 3 (order processing) to the mentor session

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 6 design notes exist, each with all six sections
- [ ] Every design states ≥3 explicit assumptions
- [ ] Every design has QPS/storage numbers with the arithmetic visible
- [ ] Every design names what the user sees in ≥2 failure modes
- [ ] Every design has ≥2 alternatives with a specific reason each lost
- [ ] Peer critique exchanged; responses recorded (fixed or argued, not ignored)

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Requirements | None or vague | Happy-path feature list | Explicit assumptions + non-functional targets | Assumptions questioned and defended in the peer review |
| Estimates | None | Numbers, no arithmetic | Shown arithmetic, right order of magnitude | Estimates checked against real data and corrected |
| Failure thinking | Happy path only | Failures listed | Failures + user impact + mitigation | Mitigations designed in (idempotency, DLQ, fallback), not bolted on |
| Trade-offs | One option picked | Alternatives listed | Alternatives with reasons | Choice tied to constraints, and states what would flip the decision |

## Submit

Fill in your submission note under `Cohort/<you>/Submissions/`, link the six design notes and the peer review thread, set `state: submitted`.

## Mentor review prompts

- "Where does this design fall over first — 10× traffic or 10× data?"
- "Which box on the diagram can you delete?"
- Pick one alternative and ask them to defend it as if they'd chosen it.
- On design 6: "Which of these decisions would your team actually make — and why not the 'best' one?"

## Stretch 🔸

- Turn design 3 into a running demo: a queue, a consumer with an idempotency-key store, and a test that proves duplicate delivery is harmless.
- Redo design 1 from memory in 45 minutes without notes. Compare with the original.
