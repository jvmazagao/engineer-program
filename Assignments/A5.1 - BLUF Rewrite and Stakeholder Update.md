# A5.1 — BLUF Rewrite and Stakeholder Update

← [[05 - Phase 5 - Influence and Communication]] · Catalog: [[Assignments.base]]

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Why

Engineers bury the decision in paragraph six and wonder why leadership reads only the first one. Bottom-line-up-front writing is the highest-leverage habit in the influence phase: same facts, half the length, act twice as fast.

## Brief

Two artifacts.

**1. The rewrite.** Take one of your past RFCs, design docs, or long Slack threads — pick the most recent rambling one. Rewrite it BLUF-style:

- First sentence states the decision or the ask
- Each section opens with its conclusion; evidence moves *down*, not *away*
- Target: under half the original length, nothing load-bearing lost
- Include a before/after of the opening paragraph in your submission note

**2. The stakeholder update.** A real update for a real project you're on, written for a *named* non-engineer (PM, support lead, manager). Fixed order: **Progress · Risks · Asks**.

- Bad news first
- Every ask names a person and a date
- No untranslated jargon
- Send it for real — the response (or silence) is part of the data

## Tasks

- [ ] Pick the past document; link the original
- [ ] Rewrite in BLUF; count words before and after
- [ ] Put the before/after opening paragraph in the submission note
- [ ] Name the stakeholder for the update and the decision they own
- [ ] Write the update: progress, risks, asks — bad news first, jargon translated
- [ ] Send it; note the response and any follow-up questions it triggered

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Rewrite is ≤50% of the original length and leads with the ask/decision
- [ ] Every section's first sentence carries that section's conclusion
- [ ] Update names its recipient and what they must decide or do
- [ ] Update sent to the real stakeholder; their questions noted
- [ ] Zero untranslated jargon in the update

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BLUF | Conclusion buried in the middle | Conclusion in the first paragraph | Conclusion in the first sentence | Reader could act on the first sentence alone |
| Compression | Same length, reordered | Some cutting | ≤50%, nothing load-bearing lost | Detail is skimmable in 30 s but still findable |
| Audience | Still engineer-facing | Jargon defined on demand | Written to the recipient's decisions | Pre-answers their most likely objection |
| Honesty | Risks softened | Risks listed | Bad news first, with a plan | The ask is concrete and dated — a "no" is possible |

## Submit

Fill in your submission note under `Cohort/<you>/Submissions/`, link the original, the rewrite, and the sent update, set `state: submitted`.

## Mentor review prompts

- "Read me your first sentence. Could your stakeholder forward just that and be understood?"
- "What did you cut? Was any of it load-bearing?"
- "Where did you soften a risk? Read that sentence again, out loud."

## Stretch 🔸

- Ask the stakeholder to grade the update 1–5 for clarity and name one thing to change; record it.
