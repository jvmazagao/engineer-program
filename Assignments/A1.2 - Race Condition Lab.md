# A1.2 — Race Condition Lab

## Why

Race conditions are the bugs you can't reproduce in the debugger and that fire once a month in prod. This lab makes one *reliably* reproducible on your laptop, so the mechanism — read-modify-write losing an update — stops being folklore. It's the same pattern behind "the counter is off", "inventory went negative", and "the balance withdrew twice".

## Brief

Create `dsa-from-scratch/09-race-condition-lab/` with four parts.

**Part 1 — Reproduce.** A shared counter, incremented by N threads × K iterations each (start at 8 × 100 000) with **no lock**. Run it 10 times; print final vs expected in a table. Tune N and K until the lost update appears in **at least 9 of 10 runs** — "reliably reproducible" is the deliverable, not a one-off fluke. In a code comment, walk the exact interleaving that loses an update (`x += 1` is read, add, write — not one operation).

**Part 2 — Fix with a lock.** Same program with a mutex around the increment. 10/10 correct runs. Measure and report the throughput cost versus the unsynchronized version.

**Part 3 — Fix by redesign.** Same correctness, **no lock**: pick one — single-writer via a queue, sharded counters summed at read, or your language's atomic primitive. Implement it, show 10/10 correct, and report throughput vs the locked version.

**Part 4 — `RACE-NOTES.md`.** Generalize: the bug is **check-then-act** (or read-modify-write): read a value, decide based on it, act — with a gap another thread can enter. List three shapes: `x += 1`, "if not exists then create", "check balance then withdraw". Then name **one place in a real service you own** where this pattern exists or could exist — endpoint, job, or DB write.

## Tasks

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Race reproduces in ≥ 9 of 10 runs with the documented thread/iteration counts
- [ ] Both fixes produce 10/10 correct runs across 10 executions each
- [ ] Throughput numbers for all three versions in one table, same machine/conditions
- [ ] The lost-update interleaving is written out step by step, not waved at
- [ ] Real-work example is specific (file/endpoint/function), not "somewhere in the API"

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reproduction | Rare or never loses updates | Loses updates sometimes | ≥ 9/10 reliable | Reliability tuned deliberately + interleaving documented |
| Lock fix | Missing or still racy | Correct, unmeasured | Correct + throughput measured | Also discusses granularity (per-counter vs global) |
| Redesign | Missing | Chosen but incorrect | Correct + measured | Correct, measured, and trade-offs vs the lock argued |
| Generalization | No notes | Lists shapes only | Shapes + generic real example | Specific codebase location + shipping recommendation |

## Mentor review prompts

- "Walk the two interleavings: one that loses an update, one that doesn't. What decides it?"
- "Your lock: what happens to throughput as thread count grows? Why?"
- "In your redesign, what replaced the lock's guarantee — and what can now go wrong that couldn't before?"
- "For the real-work example: is the fix a lock, an atomic DB update, or a redesign? What does our stack already give you?"
