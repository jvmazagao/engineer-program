# A1.3 — Profile and Fix

## Why

Seniors don't guess at slowness — they measure, fix, and prove it with numbers. This is the first assignment anchored in your real service, and its deliverable is a shipped change backed by before/after data: hypothesis → measurement → change → result.

## Brief

Pick a **real endpoint or scheduled job in a service you own at work** — one that's known to be slow, or that carries real traffic. Get your lead's OK before profiling anything shared.

1. **Baseline.** Capture current numbers: p50/p95 latency (or job duration) and call volume. Screenshot or export — you'll need them later.
2. **Profile.** Under realistic load (staging or prod-like fixtures — no prod experiments without approval), run a real profiler (`py-spy`, `pprof`, async-profiler, Chrome DevTools — whatever fits your runtime). Save the flamegraph/output as an artifact.
3. **Top 3 costs.** From the profile, list the three biggest costs with their share of time. Pick one that you can fix end to end.
4. **Fix.** Write the hypothesis first ("the serializer runs per-row and re-parses config each call → cache it, expect ~30% off p95"). Make the change, ship the PR.
5. **Measure again.** Same conditions, same method. Before/after table.
6. **Write-up** (`PERF-WRITEUP.md`, goes in the submission): hypothesis → measurement → change → result. Include what you'd fix next and **what surprised you**.

Honesty rule: a null result with good measurement **passes**. A "win" with no baseline doesn't.

## Tasks

- [ ] Get lead's approval; pick the endpoint/job and record why you picked it
- [ ] Capture baseline p50/p95 or duration + volume
- [ ] Profile under realistic load; save the flamegraph/output artifact
- [ ] List top-3 costs with % of time
- [ ] Write the hypothesis before changing anything
- [ ] Ship the fix (PR reviewed and merged, or documented if blocked)
- [ ] Re-measure under identical conditions; before/after table
- [ ] `PERF-WRITEUP.md`: hypothesis → measurement → change → result, plus next candidate and surprises

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Profiler artifact exists and is from a real profiler, not timing prints alone
- [ ] Top-3 costs quantified, not described ("the DB" is not a cost; "N+1 queries, 61% of time" is)
- [ ] Hypothesis written **before** the change, with a predicted effect
- [ ] Before/after measured under the same conditions, both numbers stated
- [ ] PR shipped or a concrete blocker documented; write-up readable by a teammate

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Profiling rigor | No profiler used | Ran profiler once, no artifact | Artifact + top-3 with % | Profiles under realistic load and distinguishes CPU vs I/O wait |
| Fix quality | Cosmetic or unrelated to costs | Right area, unverified | Targets a top cost, reviewed and merged | Minimal, safe change; considered side effects (cache invalidation, memory) |
| Measurement | No numbers | Before OR after only | Before/after, roughly same conditions | Same conditions + honest treatment of noise and confounders |
| Write-up | Missing | Result only | Full hypothesis→result chain | Includes surprises, next step, and transferable lesson |

## Mentor review prompts

- "What did you assume was slow that turned out fine?"
- "How do you know the improvement was your change and not a warm cache / quieter hour?"
- "What would this look like at 10× traffic? Does your fix scale or move the bottleneck?"
- "Show the top-3 list: which of the other two would you fix next, and what's the predicted win?"

## Stretch 🔸

- Add a regression guard: a benchmark test or CI check that fails if the fixed path regresses past the old number. Link it in the write-up.
