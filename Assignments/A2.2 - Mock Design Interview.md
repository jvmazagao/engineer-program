## Why

Designing on paper with infinite time and designing out loud in 60 minutes are different skills. The vague prompt and the minute-35 twist train the two things real interviews (and real incidents) actually test: scoping under ambiguity and adapting without freezing.

## Brief

**Session format — 60 min, mentor as interviewer, engineer as candidate:**

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Mentor gives a **deliberately vague prompt** (e.g. "design a parking garage system" — no scale, no users, no features defined) |
| 5–15 | Engineer extracts requirements, states assumptions, proposes scale numbers. Mentor answers questions with numbers only when asked |
| 15–35 | Core design: diagram, data model, main flows |
| ~35 | **The twist**: mentor injects a failure/scale change (e.g. "reads just went 100×", "the primary DB is unreachable 2 minutes a day", "the product now needs multi-region") |
| 35–55 | Engineer adapts the design live; failure modes discussed |
| 55–60 | Mentor gives interviewer-style feedback and a score |

The engineer must know the prompt is vague and the twist is coming — just not what or when. Mentor steers nothing; answers only what's asked.

**Scoring (mentor fills a sheet after):** requirements gathering, trade-off articulation, adaptation under the twist, communication — each 1–4 — plus an overall "mid or senior bar" call.

## Tasks

- [ ] Schedule the 60 min; agree only the topic area with the mentor (not the exact prompt)
- [ ] Do the session; think aloud throughout, including when stuck
- [ ] Mentor completes the score sheet with one "most important behavior to change"
- [ ] Write a retro note: 3 things to repeat, 1 thing to fix
- [ ] Within a week, redo the same prompt solo, timed to 45 min; compare against the live session

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Session happened with the twist injected around minute 35
- [ ] Requirements and assumptions stated before any diagram was drawn
- [ ] Mentor only answered questions — no steering, no rescue
- [ ] Score sheet exists with all four dimensions scored
- [ ] Retro note exists with concrete changes, and the solo redo demonstrates at least one of them

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scoping | Jumped straight to a solution | Asked some questions, assumptions stayed implicit | Requirements explicit, scale estimated unprompted | Also declared what's explicitly out of scope |
| Design | Hand-wavy, incomplete | Works for the happy path | Coherent data model and flows | Anticipated the *class* of problem the twist belongs to |
| Adaptation | Froze or restarted from zero | Patched locally, broke other parts | Adjusted the design coherently | Quantified the new trade-off and chose deliberately |
| Communication | Silent, then a big reveal | Talked but unstructured | Structured, checkable steps | Mentor never had to guess what was meant |

## Mentor review prompts

- Give numbers only when asked. If the candidate never asks, note it — that silence is a finding.
- At the twist, stay silent for a full 10 seconds. Let them re-anchor alone.
- In feedback, name the single behavior that would most raise the score. One, not ten.

## Stretch 🔸

- Reverse the session: you interview a peer with your own vague prompt and twist. Grading someone else exposes what interviewers actually listen for.
