# A1.5 — Deep-Dive Talk

← [[01 - Phase 1 - Technical Depth]] · Catalog: [[Assignments.base]]

## Why

Phase 1's deliverable is a deep-dive, and deep understanding that can't be transmitted is worth less. Explaining "how X actually works" to your real team — live, with questions you don't control — is the final test that you own the material.

## Brief

Pick a topic from Phase 1 you can ground in your own stack: your runtime's execution model, B-trees and indexes, your DB's isolation and locking, the event loop, connection pooling, how your queue delivers and retries messages. Title it **"How X actually works"**.

**Deliverables:**

- Slides (≤ 10) or a written note, published internally. Must contain: **one diagram** (yours, from A1.1 or improved), **one real example from your own codebase or services**, and **one "what this means for us" takeaway** — an action, a gotcha, or a config we should check.
- A **15-minute talk to your team** + ~10 min Q&A.

**Session format:**

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Prep | Draft materials; rehearse once with your mentor (they'll cut, probe, and ask the hostile questions early) |
| Live | The team talk itself. Mentor attends (in person or remote) and scores; one peer scores independently |
| Scoring | Mentor + peer score all rubric rows 1–4; averages go in the submission note with their notes |

If the team slot lands more than a week after your rehearsal, the scored session is the rehearsal (mentor + 1 peer) — deliver to the team when scheduled and note it.

## Tasks

- [ ] Pick topic and scope it: what's in, what's explicitly out
- [ ] Build materials: ≤ 10 slides or one note, with your diagram, a real codebase example, and the takeaway
- [ ] Rehearse once with mentor; apply their cuts
- [ ] Deliver 15 min + Q&A to the team
- [ ] Collect two independent scorings (mentor + peer) and the notes
- [ ] Publish materials internally; link in the submission note

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Talk lands within 15 minutes (± 2) including transitions
- [ ] Materials contain all three required elements — diagram, real example, takeaway
- [ ] Every rubric row scored by both mentor and peer; averages recorded
- [ ] Questions answered or explicitly deferred ("I don't know, I'll find out") — no bluffing
- [ ] Materials published internally with the takeaway readable standalone

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Only insiders could follow | Correct but rambles or over-runs | New teammate follows the arc | Every section earns its minute; the takeaway lands |
| Accuracy | Contains real errors | Mostly right, hand-waves mechanisms | Claims correct and grounded | Correct, and unknowns are named as unknowns |
| Handling questions | Deflects or bluffs | Answers only the easy ones | Handles most; admits gaps honestly | Turns hard questions into sharper explanations |
| Materials | Walls of text / decorative slides | Right content, weak structure | Diagram + example + takeaway present | Materials work standalone — someone absent still gets it |

## Mentor review prompts

- In the rehearsal: "What do I cut? Which slide confused you even though you wrote it?"
- "Which question are you hoping nobody asks? Prepare it — it's coming."
- "What's the one sentence you want people to leave with? Say it in the first two minutes too."
- "Where did you almost present a mechanism you haven't verified? Go verify it before the talk."

## Stretch 🔸

- Turn the talk into the Phase 1 published write-up: expand the note into a full deep-dive doc and circulate it beyond your team.
