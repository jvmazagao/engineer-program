# A2.4 — RFC Reviewer

← [[02 - Phase 2 - System Design]] · Catalog: [[Assignments.base]]

| Phase | Due | Format | Effort | Reviewed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 — System Design | end of week 28 | ✍️ written | ~3 h | mentor |

## Why

Writing designs teaches you your own blind spots; reviewing others' teaches you everyone's. A senior is trusted to catch the flaw in someone else's plan before it becomes everyone's problem.

## Brief

Review 2 RFCs under review at work (or two peer designs from A2.1 if work is dry). Tell the authors you'll review — no drive-by surprises.

Read for, in order: is the problem real and quantified · are goals and non-goals stated · does the proposal match the problem · are the alternatives real or straw men · what breaks at 10× · what's missing (migration, observability, rollback, cost).

Leave **≥5 substantive comments per RFC**, each labelled:

- `blocking` — must be resolved before a decision
- `suggestion` — should be considered, author's call
- `question` — needs an answer, not a change

"Substantive" means the author changed something or argued back. Typo fixes don't count toward the five.

For each `blocking` comment, state what would change your mind — or it's an opinion, not a review.

## Tasks

- [ ] Pick 2 RFCs; tell the authors you're reviewing and by when
- [ ] Read fully before commenting; note your first reaction, then verify it before posting
- [ ] Leave ≥5 substantive comments per RFC, each labelled blocking/suggestion/question
- [ ] Each blocking comment states what would change the reviewer's mind
- [ ] Log both reviews in your review log (doc, issues found, categories)
- [ ] Follow up: record what the author did with each comment

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] 2 RFCs reviewed with ≥5 substantive comments each
- [ ] Every comment labelled
- [ ] ≥1 comment per RFC catches a design-level issue (failure mode, missing migration, unquantified claim) — not style
- [ ] Every blocking comment states what would change the reviewer's mind
- [ ] Follow-up recorded: which comments landed, which were argued down, and who was right

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Depth | Typos and "+1"s | Some real comments | Catches a design flaw | Catches the flaw that would have failed in production |
| Labelling | None | Labels mostly right | Labels right with severity justified | Right severity *and* right tone for each audience |
| Fairness | Attacks the design | Nitpicks the surface | Steelmans the design before objecting | Objects only to the strongest version of the argument |
| Follow-through | Never looked back | Checked the replies | Tracked comments to resolution | Extracted a lesson about your own review blind spots |

## Submit

Fill in your submission note under `Cohort/<you>/Submissions/`, link both review threads and the follow-up notes, set `state: submitted`.

## Mentor review prompts

- "Which comment are you most confident in? Which least — and why did you post it anyway?"
- "Did you review the design or the document formatting?"
- Walk through one comment you labelled blocking that the author pushed back on. Who was right?

## Stretch 🔸

- Review a third RFC from a domain you know nothing about. Note precisely what you could and couldn't catch without domain knowledge.
