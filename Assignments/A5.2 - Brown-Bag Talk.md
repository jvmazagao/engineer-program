# A5.2 — Brown-Bag Talk

## Why

If a senior can't transfer what they know to a room, the knowledge dies with them. Twenty minutes in front of peers is the cheapest place to learn that speaking is a craft — pacing, structure, and surviving questions.

## Brief

Pick a topic you learned deeply in phases 1–4: the Phase 1 deep-dive, a postmortem, a design decision, or a debugging war story with transferable lessons. Confirm topic and slot with the mentor and get it on the team calendar.

**Session format — 30 min slot: 20 min talk + 10 min Q&A.**

Structure: **problem → why it matters to this audience → what we did/found → what's next or what to do differently.** Slides optional; a live demo or diagram beats walls of text.

Preparation requirements:

- One full rehearsal aloud, timed, cut to fit 20 minutes
- Answers prepared for the two hardest questions you hope nobody asks

**Feedback form** — paper or link, collected at the end, 4 questions:

1. Clarity (1–5)
2. Usefulness (1–5)
3. Pace (too slow / right / too fast)
4. One thing to change

Record the median scores and the most common change request in the submission note. The mentor attends and watches pace, filler words, slide-reading, and question handling.

## Tasks

- [ ] Choose the topic with the mentor; book the slot on the team calendar
- [ ] Outline: problem → why it matters → what we did → what's next
- [ ] Rehearse once aloud, timed; cut to fit 20 min
- [ ] Prepare answers for the 2 hardest expected questions
- [ ] Give the talk; run the Q&A
- [ ] Collect feedback forms; record medians and the top change request

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Talk happened with ≥3 attendees beyond the mentor
- [ ] Finished between 18 and 22 minutes (timed)
- [ ] Feedback form collected; clarity and usefulness medians recorded
- [ ] Top change request noted, with what you'd do about it
- [ ] Materials linked in the submission note

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Structure | Stream of consciousness | Topic clear, no arc | Problem → why → what → next holds | Audience knows what to *do* differently by the end |
| Delivery | Read slides, over time | On time but stiff | On time, natural pace | Absorbed an interruption without losing the thread |
| Depth | Surface recall | Correct but thin | Answers follow-ups with specifics | Says "I don't know" cleanly and commits to following up |
| Feedback | Not collected | Collected, ignored | Medians recorded | Feedback visibly drove a concrete change afterwards |

## Submit

Fill in your submission note under `Cohort/<you>/Submissions/`, link the materials, the feedback summary, and the median scores, set `state: submitted`.

## Mentor review prompts

- "Which minute dragged? What would you cut next time?"
- "You were asked X and deflected. What would a real answer have cost you?"
- "What did the audience do differently after this — anything?"

## Stretch 🔸

- Record the talk and watch 5 minutes of yourself; note two delivery habits to fix. Or give the talk again to a second team.
