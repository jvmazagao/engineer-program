# A3.4 — Game Day

## Why

Incident response is a performance skill — reading about incident command doesn't survive contact with a confusing dashboard at minute 4. A game day is where you find out what your runbooks, dashboards, and nerves actually do under pressure.

## Brief

**Session format — 2 hours: 15 min setup, 90 min exercise, 15 min debrief.**

**Before:** engineer prepares — staging access, dashboards and runbooks open, roles declared (incident commander = the engineer; mentor plays ops and optionally a confused support engineer asking "is the app down?"). Scope agreed: staging only, nothing touches prod, abort criteria named.

**The injection:** the mentor secretly injects ONE failure in staging — kill a dependency, fill a disk, add 3s latency to the DB, expire a certificate — *or* runs a tabletop ("09:14, the primary DB fails over; 30 seconds later your queue consumer starts crashing"). The engineer does not know which failure, or when.

**The exercise:** the engineer runs the incident as commander:

- Declares severity within the first 10 minutes
- Assigns roles explicitly
- Posts a written status update within the first 15 minutes, then on a fixed cadence — even when there's nothing new ("investigating X, next update in 15 min")
- Mitigates first, root-causes later (rollback, flag, scale)
- Maintains a live timeline as events happen, not reconstructed after

**Scoring (mentor):** time-to-declare, time-to-first-external-comms, time-to-mitigation, role handling, timeline quality — each 1–4.

**Debrief:** what the dashboards/runbooks failed to provide (feeds A3.3); each finding becomes an assigned action item.

## Tasks

- [ ] Schedule the 2 h; agree scope and abort rules (staging only)
- [ ] Prepare: roles assigned, comms channel open, timeline doc ready
- [ ] Run the exercise as incident commander against an unknown injection
- [ ] Post status updates on a cadence, including when nothing has changed
- [ ] Debrief with the mentor; fill the score sheet together
- [ ] Convert findings into ≥2 action items, each assigned

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Failure injected without the engineer knowing the details beforehand
- [ ] Severity declared within 10 minutes of first symptom
- [ ] First written status update within 15 minutes; cadenced updates after
- [ ] Mitigation attempted before root-causing
- [ ] Timeline maintained live, not reconstructed
- [ ] ≥2 action items from the debrief, each with an owner

## Rubric (1–4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mitigation time | Flailed or took long detours | Found the cause slowly, then fixed | Mitigated before full diagnosis | Had a rollback/flag path ready and used it |
| Comms | Nothing until solved | A single update | Cadenced updates, honest about unknowns | Updates usable by support/leadership without translation |
| Roles | Did everything alone | Delegated reluctantly | Clear IC/ops/comms split | Kept the IC role while coaching others through theirs |
| Timeline | None | Reconstructed afterwards | Live and complete | Would drop straight into a postmortem unchanged |

## Mentor review prompts

- Mid-exercise, inject socially too: "A customer just tweeted about this. What do you tell them?"
- In the debrief: "At which minute were you flying blind — and which signal would have fixed that?"
- "What would this incident have looked like at 3am instead of a Tuesday afternoon?"

## Stretch 🔸

- Run the same scenario again with a peer as IC while you mentor them through it. Compare their scores to yours.
